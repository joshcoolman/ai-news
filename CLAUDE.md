## What this is

A Node server and plain HTML, JS and CSS, with no runtime dependencies and no build step. Every file runs exactly as written: `node server.js`. The reading path is `package.json`, `server.js`, `routes.js`.

## Rules

1. **The outside world goes through one module each:** YouTube `server/youtube.js`, data `server/store.js`, Claude `server/ai/client.js`. The YouTube module leans on two undocumented playlist ids (its header says which); the store and the model provider are meant to be swappable.
2. **Every prompt is a file in `prompts/`,** loaded at call time by `server/ai/prompts.js`. Guidance is passed word for word, never rewritten into rules.
3. **Rules that could silently corrupt the feed stay pure and tested** (`server/feed/rules.js`, `browser/feed/live.js`).
4. **The folder says where code runs.** Only `browser/` is ever served, so nothing in `server/` can reach a page. Neither side imports the other: shapes they share are in `types.d.ts`, numbers they share arrive in `/api/config`.
5. **Every write goes through `store.mutate`:** a refresh that runs for minutes must not overwrite a removal made while it ran.
6. **`dependencies` stays empty and nothing is compiled.** That is the experiment (#21). A new need is written here, in the open, or argued in an issue first. `typescript` and `@types/node` only check the code: `pnpm check` and `pnpm test` are the gate where another repo would run a production build.
7. **A page is one state object and a `draw` function.** Components are functions that return elements built with `h` (`browser/shared/dom.js`); an action changes the state and calls `draw`; lists go through `sync` so unchanged cards are not touched. Attach handlers through `h` or `addEventListener`, not `el.onkeydown = ...`: a property handler that returns false cancels the event, which once swallowed Enter in the search box.
8. **A shortcut is listed where the user can find it.** Keys and clicks that have no button are described in the guide on the Settings page (`GUIDE` in `browser/settings/settings.js`); adding or changing one means updating it there.
9. **Names follow the UI's words.** Glossary:
   - **Card**: one tile in the feed, a video or a story. A refresh adds only stories; a video reaches Home only through a search or More like this.
   - **Batch**: the cards one refresh added; the newest batch carries the New tag.
   - **Favorite**: a copy of a card on the Favorites page; deleting it and deleting the feed card are independent.
   - **Name**: a specific thing the week's creator titles and chapter lines are about (`prompts/extract-names.md`; a roundup's chapters are its headlines). Each name gets one quick search for a page (`prompts/define.md`: the maker's news page first, else one web search; the progress line calls them "searches"). The creators are the signal, never the source.
   - **No source yet**: a story card whose search found no page. The name on its block, what the creators said as its title; clicking it runs the home-page Search for the name instead of opening a page.
   - **Search**: the home-page box; a YouTube search of the last month whose first 8 unseen results land at the top as a new batch (meta line `Search: <query> ·`).
   - **More like this**: a YouTube search seeded by one card, its results placed after it.
   - **Members only**: a video only a channel's paying members can play. Left out of everything the app finds (refresh, Creators page, searches) unless Settings turns that off; the one rule is `playable` in `server/feed/rules.js`.
   - **Player** (Josh says "sidecar"): the one window thumbnails play in (`/player`, named `ainews-player`), with its bar: creator or a green Add creator, a favorite star that follows the on-favorite setting (unstar undoes its own removal), and a History column right of the video, always showing, of what the Player has played and what is queued (a new video joins at the top, replaying one keeps the order; each row has a remove ×; removing the one playing moves to the next). Coming back to a video picks it up where it was left. History, finished marks and positions are localStorage: they outlive the window, and a row leaves only by its × (no cap, no expiry, #47). Each opening of the window is a session; an "Earlier" divider sits above the first row an earlier one left (`earlierFrom` in `queue.js`, #48). Never loads youtube.com itself: that wipes the window name and the next click opens a second window. It tells open pages what it changed over a BroadcastChannel.
   - **Queue**: plain-click a video on Home, Creators or Favorites while the Player is playing. The video lands in History right under the one playing; when a video ends, the Player starts the next row not yet watched to the end (`browser/player/queue.js`). On Home the card leaves for good (played or queued; a click there is a decision to watch); on Creators and Favorites it stays and wears a Queued tag (Playing, for the one playing) for as long as it is in History and ignores further clicks, and shift-click takes it out of the queue again (on the playing one, the next plays). A favorite stays a favorite. No setting, no button. With no Player playing, a click plays. The Player says when it is playing ("playing" on load and in answer to a page's "ping", "closed" on leaving); the Queued marks are read from History itself in localStorage (`browser/shared/history-link.js`), so the Player's own × and Delete, and rows queued before a page opened, count too (#52).
   - **One window** (`/`, `browser/app/`, #60; Home itself is `/home`, and `/app` still answers): the app in a left frame, the Player in a right frame named `ainews-player` (the name thumbnails open into, so a click loads that frame instead of opening a window), a rail between with a show/hide handle (localStorage `app-player-shown`; a video loading into a hidden Player shows it). F on the video fills the display. The windowed Player still opens when a page is used on its own (`/home`). A Player opened with no video (a reload) comes back to the last video it played, else the newest History row, loaded paused (`restore=1`); only an empty History shows the empty message.
   - **Player keys**: bare arrows, Space and Delete, in the Player (`browser/player/player.js`). Space also works on Home, Creators and Favorites outside inputs (`toggleOnSpace`, sent as a `toggle` message; `toggle` in `frame.js` pauses or plays by the tracked state, #61). Up and down play the row above or below in History, stopping at the ends. Left and right skip 10 seconds back or forward. Delete (or Backspace) removes the playing row, as its × does.
   - **Centered History**: the playing row sits at the column's vertical middle, a little larger than the rest, and stepping slides the list under it (`history.js` centers it on draw; `player.css` scales it; a view transition morphs the rows across the page load in Chromium browsers).
   - **Highlight**: the card on Home or Creators the keyboard is on, outlined in green (`picked` in `browser/feed/feed.js` and `browser/creators/creators.js`; rules in `pick.js`). On Creators, Left from the sidebar enters the grid at the right-edge card nearest the picked creator's row on screen (`nearestPick`, #58), Right off its right edge or Escape leaves it, Enter is the card's queue or play (unqueue on a marked card), and changing the creator drops it (#56). Arrows move it, the first press to the first card; Enter is the card's More like this and × (topic) or its click (video); Delete is its ×. When its card leaves it moves to the next, the first from the last. Escape drops it, a click moves it.
   - **Picked creator**: the creator chosen in the Creators sidebar, outlined in green; the grid shows their whole cached list (the newest 30, `FEED_CAP`) whatever the day slider says and the label reads Latest 30 (#54). While one is picked, up and down move the pick through the list, wrapping; with none picked the arrows do nothing. Clicking it again, or empty page, drops the pick (`browser/creators/creators.js`).
   - **Chapters**: a button in the Player's bar (and the C key), shown only when the video's description lists chapters. It opens a list over the History column; picking one jumps there and the list stays open until ×, Escape or a click elsewhere; while it is open, up and down walk the chapters instead of the videos (`browser/player/chapters.js`, `parseChapters` in `server/youtube-parse.js`).
   - **Add your keys**: the first screen when the server has no keys of its own (`browser/shared/keys.js`). Keys live in this browser's local storage and ride each request as headers; Settings has Delete keys.
